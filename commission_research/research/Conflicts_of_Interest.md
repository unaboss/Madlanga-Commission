# Conflicts of Interest Register — Institutional Failure Analysis

## Structural Conflicts Identified in IDAC and NPA

### 1. IDAC Staffed by Secondments from Institutions It Investigates

**Source:** NPA website — IDAC page  
**Detail:** IDAC includes "secondments from other law enforcement agencies, such as South African Police Services (SAPS), State Security Agency (SSA), Directorate for Priority Crime Investigations (DPCI), South African Revenue Services (SARS), and Independent Police Investigative Directorate (IPID)."  
**Conflict:** IDAC's mandate is to investigate corruption in these same institutions. Investigators are borrowed from the very bodies they are meant to scrutinize. A SAPS officer seconded to IDAC would be investigating SAPS colleagues. An SSA agent would investigate SSA. This creates inherent loyalty conflicts, information-sharing risks, and potential for investigations to be compromised.

### 2. IDAC Head Reports to NDPP Who Is Accountable for NPA Budget

**Chain:** Adv Andrea Johnson (IDAC Head) → Adv Andy JL Mothibi (NDPP)  
**Conflict:** IDAC is meant to be an independent anti-corruption unit within the NPA. But its head reports directly to the NDPP, who is the Accounting Officer for the NPA's entire budget — including IDAC's. If IDAC investigates corruption within the NPA itself, or if the NDPP is implicated in any matter, the structural independence is compromised. The NDPP controls IDAC's resources, appointments, and funding.

### 3. Mothibi Named in Johnson's Resignation Alongside Ramaphosa

**Source:** News24  
**Detail:** Johnson asked both President Ramaphosa AND NDPP Mothibi for permission to resign with immediate effect.  
**Conflict:** The NDPP is meant to be independent (Chapter 9 institution). If the Head of IDAC coordinates her resignation with both the President (political head) and the NDPP (her direct superior), it suggests the NDPP is not operating independently from political influence.

### 4. NPA Audit Failures While Overseeing Anti-Corruption

**AGSA Finding (15 Oct 2025):** No entity in the Justice portfolio — which includes the NPA — had a clean audit.  
**Conflict:** The NPA is tasked with prosecuting corruption, including financial crimes, while failing its own financial audits. The institution that holds others accountable for financial misconduct cannot itself pass an audit. This undermines its credibility and suggests internal financial controls are as broken as those of the institutions it investigates.

### 5. Commission Witness Claimed IDAC Operates Outside Its Mandate

**Source:** Commission Transcripts, Day 2  
**Detail:** A witness testified they had "sources within IDAC" indicating "activities that are happening with IDAC that are not in line with what the purpose of [IDAC]."  
**Conflict:** Internal whistleblowers within IDAC suggested the unit was operating beyond its legal mandate — while the Head of IDAC was simultaneously defending against these allegations at the commission. Justice Madlanga himself asked whether IDAC had "arrogated to itself the power to be an overseer over SAPS."

### 6. Commissioner (Justice Madlanga) Questioned IDAC Overreach

**Source:** Commission Transcripts, Day 141  
**Detail:** Justice Madlanga asked Colonel Padayachee (IDAC investigator): *"So, Colonel, IDAC has arrogated to itself the power to be an overseer over SAPS. Is that what you are saying?"*  
**Conflict:** The Chair of the commission — tasked with investigating corruption — explicitly questioned whether IDAC had exceeded its authority. This suggests even the commission recognized IDAC was operating beyond its statutory mandate.

### 7. Potential Cross-Institutional Recurrence (Mashazi Pattern)

**Status:** Unconfirmed — further research needed  
**Detail:** If the same pattern identified with Imogen Mashazi (moving from failed municipality to failed national department) recurs with other IDAC/NPA officials, it would suggest a systematic shuffling of failed leadership between anti-corruption bodies.
